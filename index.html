The seat math

From 2027 the World Athletics Council must seat 13 women and 13 men. Thirteen of its 26 seats are settled before the last vote. The other 13, the individual seats, are then split so the totals come out even.

Two things are not yet known: who will be elected President, and whether any Area elects a woman as its President. Move the controls to see how each one changes the split.

Seats available, not a prediction. As of November 19, 2026, all six Area associations are led by men, one of them on an interim basis. The Area elections in 2027 can change that, and each woman elected moves one seat.

How the count works

The President and Vice Presidents are elected first. The women and men already seated are then counted: the President, the four Vice Presidents, the six Area Presidents and the two athlete members. Each count is subtracted from 13, and the result is announced before the last vote.

Every federation votes for exactly 13 names, and a ballot is invalid unless it includes at least the announced number of women and of men. The seats go to the highest vote-getters of each sex.

With today's Area leaders, nine or ten of the 13 individual seats are reserved for women and three or four for men. USATF sends one nominee into that vote.

Sources: World Athletics Constitution, Articles 36.6, 41.1 and 86.1, and Rules of Congress, Rule 8.3.

Straight answers

Three questions delegates are asking, answered plainly.

What happened in 2014, and what has changed?

In 2014 the USATF Board named her as its nominee for the IAAF Council after delegates at the Annual Meeting had voted for another candidate. The Board had the authority to do that under the rules of the time. Many members felt their vote had been set aside, and that reaction was fair.

The rule has changed since, and she helped negotiate the change. Today the delegates elect the nominee, and the Board can overturn their choice only by a two-thirds vote for good cause. She is asking for that vote.

What will be different from 2018?

In 2018 the delegates chose another nominee. The lesson was plain: the case has to be made in person, in the rooms where delegates decide. This year she is making it committee by committee, with her commitments in writing and dated so members can check them.

The election itself is different too. From 2027 the Council must seat 13 women and 13 men, which is why most of the 13 individual seats will go to women.

Why her?

The arithmetic. With today's Area Presidents, nine or ten of the 13 individual seats will go to women, and USATF sends one nominee.

The record. She has won this vote before, with 163 votes in 2015, the most of any candidate for an individual seat. She served on the Council from 2015 to 2019 and chairs the World Athletics Gender Leadership Taskforce today.

The commitment. She has put in writing how and when she will report back to members.
