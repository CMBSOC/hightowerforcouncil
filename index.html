The seat math

USATF chooses its nominee first, and it can nominate only one candidate. The World Athletics Congress then elects the Council. From 2027, the full Council must include 13 women and 13 men.

If all six Area Presidents are men, nine or ten of the 13 individual seats will be allocated to women, depending on who is elected President. The Area elections can change that split.

These are available seats, not odds of winning. The number of candidates and support from voting federations will also matter. In 2023, women won 11 of the 13 individual seats.

Seats available, not a prediction. As of November 19, 2026, all six Area associations are led by men, one of them on an interim basis. The Area elections in 2027 can change that, and each woman elected moves one seat.

How the count works

The President and Vice Presidents are elected first. The women and men already seated are then counted: the President, the four Vice Presidents, the six Area Presidents and the two athlete members. Each count is subtracted from 13, and the result is announced before the last vote.

Every federation votes for exactly 13 names, and a ballot is invalid unless it includes at least the announced number of women and of men. The seats go to the highest vote-getters of each sex.

Sources: World Athletics Constitution, Articles 36.2, 36.6, 41.1 and 86.1, and Rules of Congress, Rule 8.3.

What she will work on

For athletes: a fair, clear road to LA28 and beyond

The LA28 qualification system was published on November 10, 2026. About 40 percent of athletes are expected to qualify by entry standard and 60 percent by world ranking. Achieving an entry standard does not automatically guarantee a quota place. Her focus is to make the rules clear now and fair for athletes in the next cycle.

Qualification

Plain-language guidance on how places are earned, and written athlete input before any review of the rules.

Rankings

A request for a published, worldwide review of how national and collegiate championships are rated, so that more of the meets athletes already compete in can count.

Formats

Advocacy for athlete consultation before format changes and publication of trial results.

Source: World Athletics, LA28 qualification system, November 10, 2026.

Connect every USATF voice

Regular communication, clear follow-through and an opportunity for members to be heard. If elected to the World Athletics Council in 2027, Stephanie will make these commitments part of her service.

  • Within 14 days of every Council meeting

    A written update to USATF members on public Council decisions and what they mean for the sport.

  • Four times a year

    A call with the leaders of every USATF committee, with notes shared within 14 days.

  • Every Annual Meeting

    A standing report to delegates, with time for questions.

  • At the USATF Board table

    The Council member from the United States holds a voting seat on the USATF Board. If elected to the World Athletics Council in 2027, Stephanie will bring written committee questions to that table and report back on the responses, respecting Board confidentiality.

Council service carries a duty to the sport worldwide. Updates will respect confidentiality and collective decisions, and will not disclose individual members’ votes.

Source for the Board seat: World Athletics Constitution, Article 48.1.

Straight answers

Questions about her candidacy and the USATF nomination.

Why Stephanie?

She brings experience leading USATF, service on the IAAF Council from 2015 to 2019, and continuing international work as Chair of the World Athletics Gender Leadership Taskforce.

She has won an individual Council seat before, with 163 votes in 2015. Her commitments set out how she will listen to USATF members and report back.

The 2027 election rules are relevant to USATF’s choice, but they do not guarantee any candidate a seat.

Who chooses USATF’s nominee?

The delegates do. They elect the nominee at the Annual Meeting. Under Article 17-F, the Board may reject that nominee only by a two-thirds vote of its total non-conflicted members for good cause to disqualify the individual. The nominee has a right of appeal.

Stephanie helped negotiate that two-thirds rule. She is asking delegates for their vote.

Source: USATF 2026 Governance Handbook, Articles 17-B and 17-F. Her part in negotiating the rule is Stephanie’s account.

Why now?

The international election rules are changing: the Council must reach gender parity in 2027, with two women and two men elected as Vice Presidents. As things stand, nine or ten of the 13 individual seats will go to women, and USATF can nominate only one candidate.

Her platform puts five commitments in writing, with specific reporting commitments and priorities for athletes, officials and volunteers.
